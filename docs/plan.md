# CER Coach Plan (Summary)

The full working plan is in Mr. B's CER Coach plan doc. This is the short version the code follows.

## Goal

Given only a question and sources, a student can plan, write and check a CER from memory.

## The Six Steps (students memorize these)

Read the question. Find the evidence. Answer it. Prove it. Explain why. Check it.

## Fade Rules

| Level | What the student sees |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Model | Full questions, starters on the page, word banks, a worked example on a different question |
| 2. Guided | Full questions; starters behind a Stuck? button; example on request; push-back after each station |
| 3. Prompted | Only the move cue; the full question behind a button; the student is asked to self-question before the Coach's push-back |
| 4. Solo | Move cues only; no inline push-back; the Coach's checks run only at the Check station |

- Levels are tracked separately for Claim, Evidence and Reasoning. Reasoning starts one level lower.
- Up one level: two CERs in a row where the Coach's checks pass for that move, the student's self-score is 3 or higher, and fewer than two Stuck taps.
- Down one level: the Coach finds a problem and the student did not rate it 3 or higher. If the student rated it 3 or higher, the level holds (they misjudged; that is the conversation to have).
- Self-score comes before the Coach's checks, so students practice judging their own work.

## Research Base

- De La Paz (2005), *Journal of Educational Psychology*: historical reasoning plus SRSD argument planning improved 8th graders' essays across ability groups.
- De La Paz, Monte-Sano et al. (2017), *Reading Research Quarterly*: a year-long historical writing apprenticeship raised struggling readers' historical writing.
- Wissinger (2012, De La Paz advisor): argumentative discussion with critical questions improved evidence use and rebuttals.
- Reisman (2012), *Cognition and Instruction*: sourcing, contextualization and corroboration raised historical thinking and reading comprehension.
- SRSD (Harris and Graham; IRIS Center; WWC): background, discuss, model, memorize, support, independent performance; goal-setting, self-monitoring, self-talk.
- McNeill et al. (2006), *Journal of the Learning Sciences*: faded CER scaffolds produced stronger unscaffolded reasoning.
- Martin et al. (2019), *Instructional Science*: fading works best when calibrated to each student and paired with teacher check-ins.
- Graham and Perin (2007), *Writing Next*: strategy instruction, product goals and peer collaboration are among the largest effects.
